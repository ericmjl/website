Interference: shaping a 3-qubit distribution

Path 1 adds a flat +1 to every outcome. Path 2 adds a signed pattern, scaled by α. Where they agree they reinforce; where they oppose they cancel.

paths reinforce (same sign)paths cancel (opposite sign)
Combined amplitude (1 + α·sign)
Probability = amplitude²

Probability is the squared amplitude, so canceled outcomes vanish entirely. Sweep α across zero and watch the red (opposite-sign) outcomes disappear while the blue ones brighten, then swap as you cross to the other side. Classical probabilities cannot cancel; only signed amplitudes can. That is interference, and it is how a quantum program concentrates probability on the answer.
